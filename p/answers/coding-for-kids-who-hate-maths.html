Can my kid code if they're not good at maths?

This belief keeps a lot of children away from coding unnecessarily. The overlap is real but much narrower than its reputation.

What coding actually needs

For everything a child will do in their first few years: counting, basic arithmetic, and comparing numbers. That is close to the whole list. Algebra, trigonometry and calculus appear in specific domains - graphics, simulation, machine learning - not in general programming.

What it does share with maths

The genuine overlap is structural thinking: breaking a problem down, being precise, following logic through several steps. A child who dislikes arithmetic drills may be very good at this and never have been given the chance to notice.

It often runs the other way

Plenty of children who disliked maths become more comfortable with it through coding, because a variable in a program is visibly useful in a way that x on a worksheet is not. Making it concrete changes the relationship.

Start somewhere non-numeric

Web pages, simple games and text-based programs all teach the core concepts with almost no arithmetic. If a child has decided they are 'bad at maths', do not open with a numbers lesson.
