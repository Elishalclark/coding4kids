How do I teach my kid to code if I don't know how?

This is the single most common worry parents have, and it is largely unfounded. Teaching coding and knowing coding are different skills, and the second one is optional.

Your job is not to explain, it is to protect the time

The most valuable thing a parent does is make coding a regular, unremarkable part of the week - the same way piano practice happens whether or not the parent plays piano. Consistency beats expertise. Twenty minutes, three times a week, will outperform a heroic Saturday every time.

Use something that checks the work for you

The reason parents get stuck is that they cannot tell whether the code is right. Pick a platform that checks the answer itself and explains why something failed. Then a wrong answer becomes feedback instead of a question you cannot answer.

Learn the three questions

You can unblock almost anything without reading code. Ask: What did you expect to happen? Then: What actually happened? Then: What is the smallest thing you could change? This is genuinely how professional programmers debug, and it works when you do not understand a single line on the screen.

Let them teach you

Ask your child to explain what their program does. Explaining is where understanding gets built, and it costs you nothing but attention. If they cannot explain it, they have not learned it yet - and now you both know.

What not to do

Do not sit and read the lesson ahead of them so you can stay in front. You will burn out and they will notice. Do not solve the bug for them even when you spot it. And do not treat a failed quiz as a discipline issue; it is data.
