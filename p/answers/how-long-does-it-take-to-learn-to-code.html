How long does it take a kid to learn to code?

The honest answer depends on what you mean by learning to code, so it helps to break it into milestones that are actually observable.

First working program: one session

A child should get something running on day one. If a platform takes weeks to reach a first result, that is a problem with the platform.

Comfortable with the basics: 3-6 months

At two or three short sessions a week, most children take a few months to be genuinely comfortable with sequencing, variables, loops and conditions. This is the stage where they stop needing to be told what to do next in a lesson.

Building their own things: 6-12 months

The real milestone is when a child starts a project you did not assign. This is when it stops being a subject and becomes a capability. Timing varies enormously and depends far more on motivation than aptitude.

Fluent enough to be useful: years

Same as any skill worth having. But 'useful' arrives much earlier than 'fluent' - a child who can automate something small or build a working web page has already crossed a real line.

What speeds it up most

Frequency over duration, projects they chose themselves, and not stalling on a lesson that is too hard for a week.
