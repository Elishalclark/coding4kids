My kid gets frustrated and quits. What do I do?

Almost every child hits this, and it is rarely about grit. Coding has an unusually harsh feedback loop: it fails completely and says nothing useful. That is hard for adults too.

First, check the difficulty

Frustration that appears suddenly usually means the jump between two lessons was too large. Going back one step and re-doing something they can already do is not a waste of time - it rebuilds the sense that this is solvable, which is the thing that actually broke.

Normalise the error message

Children often read an error as a verdict on them. It helps enormously to say out loud, repeatedly, that professional programmers see errors constantly all day and that the message is the computer trying to help. Read it together. Most of the time it says exactly what is wrong.

Shrink the problem

When they are stuck, do not debug the whole program. Ask them to delete everything except the smallest piece that should work, and run that. Getting one line working restores momentum faster than any encouragement.

Stop before the collapse, not after

If you can see the session turning, end it while something still works. Ending on a success - even a small one - is what determines whether they are willing to come back tomorrow.

When to be concerned

Frustration during a hard lesson is normal. Distress at the idea of starting at all is different, and usually means the level is wrong, the sessions are too long, or it has quietly become a source of pressure between you.
