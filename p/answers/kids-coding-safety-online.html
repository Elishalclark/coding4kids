Is coding online safe for my child?

Coding platforms vary enormously on safety, and the differences are not obvious from the marketing. These are the things actually worth checking.

Public galleries

Many coding platforms let children publish projects publicly and let anyone comment. That is a genuine feature with genuine value - and also the single largest source of contact risk on these platforms. Decide deliberately whether you want it, rather than discovering it later.

Messaging between users

Ask specifically whether children can send each other messages, including through project comments or shared workspaces. 'Moderated' is not the same as 'absent', and moderation quality varies wildly.

What data is collected

For a child account, the defensible minimum is roughly: a name or nickname, a login, an age, a parent contact, and learning progress. If a platform wants more than that, it is worth asking why.

Who can approve the account

Under COPPA in the US, services aimed at under-13s need verifiable parental consent before collecting personal information. In practice, check whether the approval genuinely goes to you - or whether a child could complete it themselves.

Third parties

Read the privacy policy for what is shared, and with whom. Analytics is normal. Advertising networks on a children's product are not.
