Coding for 6 and 7 year olds: what actually works

Six is a reasonable starting age, provided the expectations match what a six-year-old can actually do.

Keep sessions to 10-15 minutes

This is the single most important adjustment. A short successful session beats a long one that ends badly, and at this age attention for a demanding task genuinely runs out around the fifteen-minute mark.

Reading level is the real constraint

Most difficulty at this age is reading, not logic. Look for lessons that use pictures and audio, and expect to read instructions aloud. A child who cannot read the instruction cannot fail the concept.

What to teach

Sequencing first: things happen in order. Then repetition. Then simple conditions. That is genuinely enough for a year. Resist the urge to accelerate because they are doing well.

What to skip

Typing real code, variables as abstract containers, and anything requiring accurate spelling. All of it can wait, and pushing it early is the most common way to make a young child decide coding is not for them.

Sit with them

At this age it is a shared activity, not an independent one. That is not a failure of the platform; it is the age.
