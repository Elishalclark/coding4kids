Is coding screen time? How much is too much?

Parents often feel a quiet guilt about adding more screen time, even for something educational. The distinction that helps most is between screens that consume attention and screens that require it.

Creation and consumption are different activities

Watching an autoplaying feed and building a working program are both 'screen time' in the sense that both involve a screen, and that is roughly where the similarity ends. One is designed to hold attention passively; the other demands sustained, effortful focus and produces something that did not exist before.

A workable limit

For most children, 20-40 minutes of focused coding is productive, and beyond about an hour the returns drop sharply - especially under 10. Frustration, sloppy guessing and clicking randomly are all signs the session has gone on too long, not signs of a bad attitude.

Watch for the good kind of absorption

There is a real difference between a child who is absorbed in solving something and a child who is stuck in a loop of trying things at random. The first is worth protecting even past your usual limit. The second should end the session.

Make the output visible

Ask to be shown what they made. It reframes the activity for both of you - it becomes something they did rather than something they were on, and it gives you an honest read on whether the time is producing anything.

Pair it with something physical

Coding sessions work better immediately before something active than immediately after. A tired child debugging is a miserable child debugging.
