Coding for kids with ADHD or who struggle to focus

Many parents are surprised to find that a child who cannot sit through twenty minutes of worksheets will happily spend an hour debugging. There are structural reasons for that.

The feedback loop is immediate

Run the code, see the result, now. There is no waiting until tomorrow to find out if you were right. For a child who struggles with delayed reward, that tightness is the whole difference.

It is genuinely interactive

Coding is not something you receive, it is something you do continuously. There is no passive stretch to drift out of. The activity itself keeps pulling attention back.

Set it up in short, finishable pieces

The single most effective adjustment is making sure every session has a visible end point that is reachable. 'Finish this lesson' works. 'Work on coding for a while' does not.

Let the interest lead

If they want to make a game, do the lessons that get to a game fastest, even out of order. Sequence matters less than momentum, and a motivated detour teaches more than a compliant march through the syllabus.

Reduce the friction to start

Same time, same place, already logged in. Most lost sessions are lost before the first line of code, in the gap between deciding to start and actually starting.
