Does homeschool computer science count toward requirements?

Requirements vary a great deal by state and country, so treat this as a guide to what to keep rather than legal advice. Check your own state's rules, because they genuinely differ.

Keep evidence, not just hours

Most reporting regimes want to see that learning happened, not just that time passed. Finished projects, completed lesson lists, quiz scores and dated certificates are all far more persuasive than a log that says '3 hours of computer science'.

What a good portfolio entry looks like

A dated record of what was covered, a sample of the child's actual work, and some form of assessment. If a platform gives you completion records and printable certificates, that is most of the entry already assembled.

Counting it as a subject

In many states, computer science can satisfy an elective, a technology requirement, or in some cases a maths credit at high-school level. The determining factor is usually rigour and hours, not the provider. Keep enough detail that you could justify the credit if asked.

For high school transcripts

If you are building a transcript, name the course something recognisable - 'Introduction to Computer Science' or 'Programming I' - rather than the brand name of the platform. List the topics covered and the hours. Admissions readers need to map it onto something familiar.

The mistake to avoid

Do not wait until reporting season to reconstruct the year from memory. Export or print records as you go. Platforms change, subscriptions lapse, and access to your own history is not guaranteed forever.
