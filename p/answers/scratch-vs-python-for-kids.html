Scratch or Python: which should my kid learn first?

This is usually framed as a rivalry, but they solve different problems. The right question is not which is better, it is which one your child needs right now.

What block coding is genuinely good at

Blocks remove two obstacles at once: typing and syntax errors. A six-year-old cannot misspell a keyword if the keyword is a shape they drag. That lets them focus entirely on logic - order, repetition, conditions - which is the part that actually transfers to every language later.

What block coding hides

Blocks make some things look easier than they are. Variables, scope and data types are all smoothed over. Kids who spend years in blocks often hit a wall when they move to text, not because text is harder, but because they never met the concepts blocks were protecting them from.

Why Python is the usual next step

Python reads closer to English than most languages, needs almost no boilerplate to do something visible, and has forgiving syntax. print("Hi") does exactly what it looks like it does. That immediacy matters enormously for a child's motivation.

A practical sequence

If your child is under 8 or is a reluctant reader, start with blocks and move to Python within a year. If your child is 8 or older and reads comfortably, start with Python directly - many kids find blocks patronising and disengage. If they have already done blocks and seem bored, that boredom is the signal to switch.

The thing that actually matters

Neither choice is permanent, and neither is a mistake. A child who enjoys coding will learn the other one in weeks. A child who is bored will not learn either. Optimise for engagement, not for the theoretically correct order.
