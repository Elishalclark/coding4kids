How to run a coding club at home or in a co-op

Group coding works well because kids unblock each other, and it removes the pressure on one adult to have all the answers.

A format that works

Ninety minutes: fifteen minutes settling and reviewing what people made last time, forty-five minutes of individual work on their own track, twenty minutes of build-anything time, ten minutes of showing. The showing is not optional - it is what makes them come back.

Handle mixed ages by decoupling

Do not teach to the middle. Put every child on their own self-paced track so a six-year-old and a twelve-year-old can sit at the same table doing completely different work. The room stays social; the difficulty stays individual.

Use the older kids

A child who has just learned something is often better at explaining it than an adult who learned it long ago. Formalise it: whoever finishes first becomes the person others ask. It is genuinely useful and it is good for them.

What the adult actually does

Timekeeping, unblocking with the three debugging questions, and making sure the quiet child is not silently stuck. You do not need to know the material.

Keep a record

For homeschool co-ops especially, note what was covered each session. It takes two minutes and saves an afternoon later.
